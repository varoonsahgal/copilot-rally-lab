The mission

The game ends. The season doesn't.

Rally Weekend will come and go, but Friday's make-good report, next month's promotions and the July 13 magnet night won't wait. Status chasing is where good teams lose hours every week.

Set up a scheduled prompt that delivers a weekly status and outstanding-commitments digest automatically. Then pick the one workflow you will improve on Monday.

☝ Call your shotPredict before you play. Wrong guesses teach the most.

Which of these is a good fit for a scheduled Copilot prompt?

Summarizing and alerting is the sweet spot. The others change business systems or make commitments. Those need a designed workflow with permissions, audit and a human in the loop.

Game plan

  • Write a recurring status and outstanding-commitments prompt.
  • Schedule it (or preview how, subject to your organization's settings).
  • Tell scheduled analysis apart from automated business actions.
  • Choose the right Copilot tool for a workflow of your own.

Play-by-play

  1. Write the digest prompt and run it once

    Run it manually first. Never schedule a prompt you haven't seen work.

    Copilot Chat
    Create my weekly Promotions status digest for the past 7 days.
Look across my email, Teams chats and meetings, and files I've worked on that mention promotions, giveaways, sponsors or [project name, e.g. Rally Weekend].
Sections:
1. Outstanding commitments: who promised what to whom, the due date, and status (Open / Overdue / Done), with a link to the source.
2. Approvals still pending: item, which approval is missing, owner.
3. Important changes since last week: numbers, dates or scope that changed, shown as old -> new, with the source.
4. Sponsor follow-ups due in the next 7 days.
5. Top 3 things I should do Monday morning.
Only include items with a source. If a section has nothing, write "Nothing found" rather than guessing.
    Classroom note

    Your mailbox doesn't contain the fictional Rally Weekend emails, so run this against your real work (swap in a real project name), or point it at the lab files: "...using the files in my Rally Weekend Task Force folder".

  2. Schedule it

    • After the prompt runs, look for the option to schedule it, usually in the menu on your prompt or response.
    • Choose a cadence (for example Mondays, 7:30 AM) and an end date if offered.
    • Results arrive in Copilot Chat, often with a notification, on each run.
    Rain delay · availability

    Scheduled prompts depend on license, release and admin settings, and may limit how many you can create or how often they run. Not available? Save the prompt to your prompt gallery or a pinned note, and put a 5-minute weekly calendar reminder on it. Same habit, one extra click.

  3. Know what a scheduled prompt does, and doesn't do

    Scheduled prompt doesScheduled prompt does not
    Read and summarize what you already have access toUpdate the ticketing, inventory or contract system
    Flag overdue commitments and pending approvalsSend emails to sponsors or fans on your behalf
    Compare this week to last weekApprove, reject or reorder anything
    Deliver results to you to reviewGuarantee it found everything (check the sources)

    Need the system to act, like creating a task or notifying a channel? That's an automated workflow (for example in Power Automate or Copilot Studio), with an owner, testing and governance.

  4. Two more recurring prompts worth stealing

    Copilot Chat · schedule daily, event week
    Every weekday at 8:00 AM during event week: summarize anything new about [event name] since yesterday (emails, chats, meetings, file changes). Flag anything that changes a quantity, date, approval or sponsor commitment. Keep it under 150 words.
    Copilot Chat · schedule Friday 3 PM
    Every Friday at 3:00 PM: list the meetings I attended this week where I was assigned an action item, with the action, due date and whether I've replied or completed it.

The big idea

Automate the asking, not the deciding

Recurring prompts keep you aware on a schedule. Judgment, approvals and changes to business systems stay with people.

1

Recurring questions are a signal

If you ask the same status question every week, schedule it. It's the cheapest automation in Microsoft 365.

2

Ask what changed

Ask for what's new, changed or overdue since last time, not a full re-summary. Lead with changes in status.

3

Read, don't write

A scheduled prompt summarizes and alerts. It doesn't update ticketing, place orders or email sponsors. Those need designed workflows and human approval.
