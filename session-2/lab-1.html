Four overnight changes. Which assumptions and recommendations no longer hold?

The mission

Thursday, 7:45 AM. The phone buzzes.

Yesterday the Task Force delivered a readiness scorecard: Friday green, Saturday with a bobblehead shortfall plan, Sunday once the Right Field pallet was rescheduled, and the bucket hats delayed. Leadership loved it.

✉ Outlook · now
Elena OrtizPLOT TWIST - Four changes overnight. I need a revised recommendation by 3:00 PM.
✉ Outlook · 11:48 PM last night
Ryan CastellanoSigned! See you Sunday 🧢

One of yesterday's actions never happened: nobody called Ryan to say "not this weekend," and at 11:48 PM he signed the redlined amendment. Sam pulled the hat line from the fan email on Wednesday, and now Jordan wants it back in before the 10:00 AM send. (Lesson one: an action item with no "done when" slips.)

Then Elena Ortiz's memo lands: "Four changes overnight." Sunday weather has turned, Big River Energy signed on their terms, the bobblehead gap now has a price tag, and the fireworks vendor added a surcharge. The fan email goes out at 10:00 AM. Elena needs a revised recommendation by 3:00 PM.

Your job this inning: figure out which of yesterday's conclusions still hold, which need to be reopened, and which questions the rest of today's toolkit has to answer.

☝ Call your shotPredict before you play. Wrong guesses teach the most.

After the overnight changes (Sunday down to 28,500, Saturday up to 41,000, and a $6,500 fireworks surcharge), does the weekend still hit the $2.4M objective?

Over by just $8,000 ($2,408,000). One more decision, such as air-freighting replacement bobbleheads for $8,100, tips it under. Thin margins turn small choices into strategic ones.

Game plan

  • Recap Session 1 findings in under a minute with Copilot.
  • Compare a new memo against earlier assumptions and spot what changed.
  • Sort recommendations into still valid, reopened, and broken.
  • Turn the changes into a short list of business questions for innings 2-6.

  3. Sort yesterday's recommendations

    Not every conclusion breaks. Make Copilot tell you which ones still stand.

    Copilot Chat
    For each recommendation in yesterday's findings, classify it as:
- STILL VALID (the memo does not affect it),
- REOPENED (the memo changes the inputs, so we have to decide again), or
- BROKEN (the memo makes it wrong).

Then list any assumption we relied on yesterday that nobody has re-checked today.

The big idea

Re-test assumptions, not just answers

When the facts change, trace which conclusions depended on them. A recommendation expires when the assumptions under it do.

1

Assumption lineage

Every recommendation rests on assumptions: Sunday at 33,000, 5,000 hats, no fix for bobbleheads. Map each conclusion to its assumptions, and a change tells you exactly what to revisit.

2

New isn't the same as good

"The sponsor signed!" sounds like progress. On their terms, it costs about $31K. A signed contract and a good deal are different things.

3

Questions before answers

When a twist lands, your first output should be the list of questions it raises, not a new recommendation. Copilot will happily skip straight to answers unless you ask.

Dugout notes

Dugout notes · instructor answer key

Timing (10 min): 2 min story setup (read the two notifications aloud) · 2 min recap prompt · 3 min comparison and triage, with step 4 as an instructor demo · 3 min challenge debrief. Run the Bullpen drill as a 1-minute room poll, or skip it.

Staffing catch: the memo says the 10,000-hat scope needs "9 extra gate staff." The Ops Playbook rule (+3 staff per dual-item gate × 5 gates) implies 15. Reward anyone who spots it with an E. Either way, Ops capacity isn't signed off.

Reference question list (ranked):

  1. What must change in the 10:00 AM fan email? (Fan Experience · fan email draft + approval status · 10:00 AM · Chat/agent)
  2. Is the BRE deal as signed acceptable, or do we counter or decline? (VP Marketing + Sponsorship · memo, budget, Ops staffing · 3 PM · simulator + agent)
  3. Air-freight 600 bobbleheads ($8,100) or offer the make-good ($4,800 credit or replacement night)? (Sponsorship + Finance · sponsor contract · Fri arrival · simulator)
  4. Does the weekend still clear the $2.4M objective? (Finance · budget model · 3 PM · simulator)
  5. Can Ops staff 10,000 hats at all five gates on Sunday? (Ops · staffing agency confirmation · today · agent flags as missing)
  6. What is our Sunday weather and rain policy for giveaways? (Fan Experience · Event Standards · today · notebook)
  7. Was the Right Field mini bat pallet rescheduled, and were the magnets pulled back? (Warehouse · delivery file · today · scheduled prompt / notebook)

Challenge answer: Take "Bucket Hat Sunday" out of the email. Even though the amendment is signed, Ops hasn't signed off and hats can't be guaranteed at every gate. Keep "first 15,000 fans" only if the air-freight is approved before send; otherwise use "while supplies last". Add a short weather note pointing to the rain policy.

Facilitation tip: The best debate is whether "signed" equals "approved". Let one group argue each side, then point to the three-part approval rule in the Ops Playbook.
