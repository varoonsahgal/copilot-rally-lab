The mission

Play ball, Task Force.

It's Wednesday, June 16, 2027, 9:00 AM. Riverfront Rally Weekend starts in 48 hours. Dana Whitfield (Promotions) says the plan is locked. Jordan Pierce (Sponsorship) says a new Big River Energy bucket hat giveaway is approved. Marcus Bell (Operations) says it isn't. The warehouse says bobbleheads are covered, but nobody has done the math.

Before you swing at anything, you need an investigation plan: what you need to know, where the evidence lives, and what would count as "confirmed." In this inning you learn the difference between asking Copilot a general question and grounding it in work evidence.

☝ Call your shotPredict before you play. Wrong guesses teach the most.

Before you open a single file: of the weekend's five promotional elements (towels, fireworks, bobbleheads, mini bats, bucket hats), how many do you think are genuinely ready?

Only 2 of 5 (towels and fireworks) survive the investigation. You'll prove it yourself by Inning 5. Most people guess 4, because the loudest messages in the thread are the confident ones.

Game plan

  • Explain the difference between a general (web) answer and a grounded (work) answer, and see it happen.
  • Choose between Copilot Chat, Researcher and Analyst for a given question.
  • Write prompts with the 5-Tool Prompt: Goal · Context · Evidence · Constraints · Output.
  • Produce a clear investigation plan for the Rally Weekend readiness question.

Rain delay · Work IQ on or off

In Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat, look for the Work IQ switch at the top left of the chat, next to the model picker (Auto). When it's on, Copilot can use your Microsoft 365 content (mail, chats, files you can access). Click it to turn it off: the label gets a strikethrough and Copilot answers without your work data. It stays off in new chats until you turn it back on. Some tenants show a Work / Web toggle instead, and some hide both. If you see neither, attach the files directly and you're grounded either way.

The play-by-play

  1. Swing #1: the ungrounded question

    Open Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat. Turn Work IQ off (click it so the label is struck through), or switch to Web if your tenant shows that toggle instead. Don't attach anything. Ask:

    Copilot Chat · Work IQ off · no files
    Is the Riverfront Rally Weekend promotion ready to go? What should we check before the weekend?
    What you should see
    • A polite, generic checklist ("confirm inventory, train staff, communicate with fans…").
    • No mention of bucket hats, Big River Energy, Jordan or Marcus. Copilot has no idea what your weekend is.
    • Possibly confident-sounding filler. That's the danger: it sounds like an answer.
    • It may even admit it needs your project files and offer a Turn on Work IQ suggestion, then hand you a full generic checklist anyway.

  2. Swing #2: the same question, grounded

    Start a New chat and turn Work IQ back on (or switch to Work). Attach 03_Event_Plan_v3…docx and 01_Email_Thread_Export.docx with the + button, or type / and pick the files from OneDrive. If you upload from your computer, Copilot tells you it saves a copy to OneDrive. Ask the same question, with one added sentence:

    Copilot Chat · Work IQ on · 2 files attached
    Is the Riverfront Rally Weekend promotion ready to go? What should we check before the weekend?
Answer only from the attached event plan and email export. For each point, cite the document or email ID (E01-E16) it comes from.
    What you should see
    • Specifics: the June 4 lock date, the three-part approval rule (E01), the bucket hat dispute (E03-E10), magnets delivered despite cancellation (E13/E14).
    • Citations you can click to open the source.
    • Probably some gaps. It may flag bobblehead damage from Priya's E12 email (25 cases) and the $8 make-good (E16), but it hasn't seen the spreadsheets, so it can't give you the real usable count or spot the duplicated delivery row.
    Scouting report · Why grounding matters

    Copilot only knows what it can reach. A grounded answer is only as complete as the evidence you point it at. Part of every investigation is asking, "What hasn't it seen?"

  3. Pick the right player: Chat vs. Researcher vs. Analyst

    Each Copilot tool plays a different position. Match the question to the tool before you start typing.

    ToolPositionUse it when…Rally Weekend exampleSpeed
    Copilot ChatCatcher · home plateQuick, focused questions; drafting; summarizing a few items you can point to."Summarize Marcus's June 2 email."Seconds
    ResearcherScout · 1BMulti-step, multi-source questions that need a structured, cited report. Can combine work and web."Is the weekend actually ready? Cross-check plans, contracts and comms."Several minutes
    AnalystAnalytics dept. · 2BData questions across one or more files: reconciling, trends, outliers, charts. Reasons step by step and can run code."Compare allocations with deliveries and list every discrepancy."A few minutes
    Copilot in ExcelGroundskeeper · 3BChanging a workbook directly: formulas, columns, formatting, PivotTables, charts."Add a Distributable Qty column = Qty - Damaged Qty."Seconds
    Quick quiz: which tool? (click to reveal)
    1. "What did Elena actually say about the bucket hats?" → Chat (one email, quick lookup).
    2. "Do bobbleheads historically lift Saturday attendance?" → Analyst (trend across 163 rows).
    3. "Write a readiness brief covering contracts, ops rules and comms, with sources." → Researcher.
    4. "Highlight every delivery row with a UOM of CS." → Copilot in Excel.

  4. Learn the 5-Tool Prompt

    A five-tool player can hit, hit for power, run, field and throw. A five-tool prompt has five parts too:

    ToolWhat it doesRally Weekend example
    1 · GoalThe decision or deliverable you need."I need to decide whether each promotional element is ready."
    2 · ContextWho you are, the situation, the deadline."I'm on the Task Force. The weekend starts Friday 6/18. A fan email sends Thursday."
    3 · EvidenceExactly which sources to use, and which not to use."Use only the attached event plan and email export."
    4 · ConstraintsRules for honesty and scope."Don't guess. Label anything unconfirmed. Cite email IDs."
    5 · OutputThe exact shape of the answer."A table with columns: Question, Source, Owner, Status."

  5. Live demo: build the investigation plan

    Keep the same two files attached (add any others from the kit you like) and run the full 5-tool prompt:

    Copilot Chat · Work
    GOAL: I need an investigation plan to decide whether each element of the Riverfront Rally Weekend (Fri 6/18 - Sun 6/20, 2027) is ready, before leadership makes a go / no-go call.

CONTEXT: I'm on the Riverfront Rally Weekend Task Force. Marketing says the plan is locked, Sponsorship says a new Big River Energy bucket hat giveaway is approved, and Operations says it isn't. Warehouse quantities may not match what was promised to sponsors. A fan email goes to 180,000 fans on Thursday 6/17 at 10:00 AM.

EVIDENCE: Use only the attached files. Do not use web results.

CONSTRAINTS: Do not decide whether anything is ready yet. Identify what information is needed to make a responsible decision. If the files conflict, say so and name both sources. Mark anything you are inferring as an assumption.

OUTPUT:
1. A table with columns: # | Question we must answer | Why it matters | Where the evidence should be (email ID, document, or system) | Who owns the answer | What would count as "confirmed"
2. A short list of the conflicting claims you already see, with sources.
3. The three questions to answer first, given the Thursday 10:00 AM fan email.
    What you should see
    • 8-15 investigation questions covering approvals, contracts, inventory/deliveries, staffing, fan comms, finance and the fireworks.
    • Conflicts already visible: E04 (conditional approval) vs E05/E06/E10 ("approved"; Copilot may cite any of them); E08 (Marcus: not signed off); E02 cancellation vs E13 delivery.
    • A "confirmed" definition that matches the three-part rule in E01: signed amendment + VP Marketing approval + Ops sign-off.
    Umpire review

    Open at least two cited emails and confirm they say what Copilot claims. Did it notice that Elena's approval says "pending"? Did it list the Excel workbooks as a needed source, even though you didn't attach them? A good plan names the evidence it hasn't seen yet.

  6. Tighten it and save it

    Iterate once, then keep the plan. You'll use it to steer Innings 2-5.

    Copilot Chat · Work
    Good. Now map each question in your plan to the Copilot tool best suited to answer it (Copilot Chat, Copilot in Outlook/Teams, Researcher, Analyst, or Copilot in Excel) and add that as a new column. Then add a final column "Deadline" using these real deadlines from the files: staffing confirmation Wed 6/16 noon, fan email content final Wed 6/16 5:00 PM, fan email sends Thu 6/17 10:00 AM.
    Umpire review · Did Researcher get benched?

    Copilot may assign no question to Researcher at all, saying this isn't an external research problem. That's a misread: as the tool table above says, Researcher can work across your work files and build a cited, multi-source report. Ask it: "Which of these questions would a cited Researcher report answer better than Chat?" Also check that Analyst isn't assigned to a simple contract lookup.

    Scouting report · Keep your plan

    Under the response, select … (More options) > Export to > Word, or use Copy Response and paste into a Word doc. If your tenant offers a Pages option, that works too. Name it Rally Weekend - Investigation Plan and save it to your Task Force folder.

The big idea

Ground the swing

Copilot is only as good as what it can see. Name the sources, state the goal, and demand evidence. Otherwise you get a confident answer to a question about someone else's company.

1

General vs. grounded

Web knowledge answers "how do promotions usually work?" Work grounding answers "what did we commit to?" Confident nonsense comes from mixing them without noticing.

2

Pick the position, then the player

Chat is fast and bounded. Researcher is multi-step and cited, and takes minutes. Analyst writes code over numbers. Picking the wrong tool wastes more time than a weak prompt.

3

Plan before you investigate

An investigation plan (questions → sources → what counts as "confirmed") turns Copilot from an answer machine into a process, and it shows the gaps before they surprise you.

📎 Take it to your desk

The same move, ready for your real work. Copy it now and use it this week.

Copilot Chat · your work
I'm about to investigate [a decision or problem at work].
Before searching anything, build me an investigation plan:
- the 5 questions I must answer
- the most likely source for each (email, meeting, file, system, or person)
- what would count as "confirmed" for each
- the one assumption most likely to be wrong
Format as a table I can work through.
