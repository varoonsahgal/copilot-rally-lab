The mission

Is the promotion really ready?

Inning 2 untangled one decision. Leadership's question is bigger: is the whole Riverfront Rally Weekend ready to proceed? That means cross-checking the event plan against sponsor contracts, the Ops playbook and two weeks of communications, all at once.

That's a job for Researcher, Copilot's cleanup hitter. It plans a multi-step investigation, reads across sources and returns a structured report with citations. Your job is to aim it well, then find where its conclusions are thinner than they look.

☝ Call your shotPredict before you play. Wrong guesses teach the most.

Researcher cites a source for every conclusion. What's the MOST likely way a cited conclusion is still wrong?

In this case file the transcript is real and citable, and it contains Jordan's overclaim and Priya's uncleaned total. Grounding gets you accurate quotes of inaccurate statements. Verification still matters.

Game plan

  • Decide when Researcher beats Copilot Chat, and when it doesn't.
  • Break a complex business question into lines of investigation.
  • Combine work sources and web research deliberately, and keep them labeled.
  • Generate a structured, cited readiness brief comparing alternatives and risks.
  • Evaluate source quality and spot unsupported conclusions.

Rain delay · Access & limits

Open Researcher from the Microsoft 365 Copilot app → Agents (or "All agents") → Researcher. It needs a Microsoft 365 Copilot license, and some tenants limit how many Researcher runs you get each month, so write your prompt carefully before you run it. Researcher may ask clarifying questions before it starts. Answer them; that's a feature. It's strongest on documents, mail and chats. For deep spreadsheet math, Analyst (Inning 4) is the better player.

The play-by-play

  1. Break the question into lines of investigation

    "Is it ready?" is too big to verify in one piece. Split it into lines Researcher can investigate separately and you can check separately:

    1 · ContractsDoes every promotion have a signed contract whose scope matches the plan?
    2 · ApprovalsHas every item passed the three-part rule before the June 4 lock?
    3 · InventoryDo delivered, usable quantities meet sponsor guarantees?
    4 · OperationsStaffing, delivery cutoffs, dock space, distribution standards.
    5 · Fan commsDoes the fan email promise only what's approved?
    6 · FinanceIs revenue counted only when contracted? What's our make-good exposure?

    You can let Researcher propose its own lines, but giving them to it makes the report easier to check and compare.

  2. Run Researcher on the readiness question

    Open Researcher. Reference the five files above (type / and pick them from OneDrive, or attach them). Then run:

    Researcher · work sources
    I'm on the Riverfront Rally Weekend Task Force. The weekend is Fri 6/18 - Sun 6/20, 2027; today is Wed 6/16, 9:00 AM. Leadership needs a promotional-readiness assessment before a fan email goes to 180,000 fans Thursday at 10:00 AM.

Investigate whether the weekend is sufficiently prepared to proceed. Use ONLY these work sources: Event Plan v3, Sponsor Requirements Summary, Operations Playbook - Giveaway Rules, the Email Thread Export (E01-E16), and the Teams Ops Huddle Transcript. Treat the auto-generated meeting recap as unreliable unless the transcript confirms it.

Investigate these lines separately:
1. Contracts - signed status and scope for each sponsor
2. Approvals - each item against the 3-part approval rule and the June 4 lock date
3. Inventory - whether usable quantities can meet each sponsor's guaranteed quantity
4. Operations - staffing, the 48-hour delivery cutoff, dock capacity, distribution standards
5. Fan communications - whether the planned email promises anything unapproved
6. Finance - revenue booked without contracts; make-good exposure

Report structure:
A. Executive summary (5 sentences max) with an overall recommendation: Proceed / Proceed with conditions / Delay - for each game day
B. Findings by line of investigation, each with a confidence level (High / Medium / Low) and citations
C. Risk table: Risk | Likelihood | Business impact | Evidence | Owner
D. Alternatives considered for any element that is not ready (at least 2 options each, with trade-offs)
E. Unresolved questions that block a confident decision, and who can answer each one

Rules: cite a specific source for every finding. Clearly separate verified facts from claims people made. If the sources do not contain enough information to conclude something, say "insufficient evidence" rather than guessing.
    Scouting report · While it runs

    Researcher shows its reasoning steps as it works, such as which sources it searched and what it's looking for next. Watch them. If it starts drifting (searching for something irrelevant), you'll learn how to tighten the prompt next time.

    What you should see
    • Friday looks ready. Sunday's bucket hat is flagged as not approved: two of three approvals are missing, scope changed by the 6/9 redline, no PO.
    • The fan email risk and the $25K booked-revenue risk.
    • Probably weak on inventory. The Word docs and emails mention damage and the "more than fifteen thousand" claim, but the actual counts live in Excel. Watch how confidently it talks about bobbleheads.

  3. Optional: add the web, deliberately

    Some questions benefit from outside context, for example how organizations typically handle weather contingency for giveaways or sponsor make-goods. If your tenant allows web grounding in Researcher, ask a separate, labeled follow-up:

    Researcher · work + web
    Follow-up: for the elements you rated "Proceed with conditions" or "Delay", research publicly available guidance on (a) how sports and live-event organizations typically communicate a giveaway that is cancelled or changed after being announced, and (b) common practices for weather contingency on giveaway days.

Keep web findings in a separate section titled "External context (web)". Never mix web sources into the internal findings. For each web source, note the publisher and why it is or isn't credible. Do not use web sources to make claims about our internal approvals, contracts, or quantities.
    Umpire review · Work vs. web

    Web context can inform options. It can never confirm an internal fact. If a web article ends up "supporting" a claim about our bucket hat approval, the report has gone off-sides.

  4. Interrogate the report: sources and confidence

    Researcher (or Copilot Chat with the report pasted)
    Review your own report as a skeptical auditor.

1. List every conclusion that rests on a single source, or on something a person SAID (in an email or meeting) rather than a document or system record.
2. For each, rate source quality: System record / Signed document / Written email from the decision owner / Verbal claim in a meeting / Second-hand claim.
3. Identify any conclusion where you were more confident than the evidence allows, and rewrite it with the right confidence level.
4. List the sources you did NOT have access to that would most change your conclusions.
    What you should see
    • "Bobbleheads are covered" turns out to rest on Priya's verbal claim at 00:01:30 ("the system shows…"), not on counted data.
    • Missing sources: the two Excel workbooks (allocations, deliveries, inventory), the contract system, and the staffing agency's confirmation.

  5. Shape it into a readiness brief

    Turn the report into a one-page brief your VP would actually read. Use the template below as the target shape.

    Copilot Chat or Researcher follow-up
    Condense the report into a one-page Promotional-Readiness Investigation Brief for Elena Ortiz (VP Marketing), using exactly these headings:
1. Bottom line (2 sentences)
2. Readiness by game day (Fri / Sat / Sun: status + one-line reason + key citation)
3. Top 5 risks (ranked by business impact)
4. Supporting sources (what we relied on, and how reliable each is)
5. Unresolved questions (question | who can answer | needed by)
6. Recommended next steps before Thursday 10:00 AM

Plain language, no jargon. Keep every citation.

    Template · Promotional-Readiness Investigation Brief

    • Bottom line: …
    • Readiness by game day: Fri Proceed · Sat ? · Sun ?
    • Top risks: 1. … 2. … 3. …
    • Sources & reliability: Signed docs · written emails · verbal claims
    • Unresolved questions: question → owner → deadline
    • Next steps: before Wed 5:00 PM / before Thu 10:00 AM

The big idea

Depth needs structure

Deep research is only as good as the questions it's broken into. Ask for the lines of investigation, the citations, and the weakest link.

1

Decompose the question

"Is it ready?" is really six questions: contracts, approvals, inventory, operations, fan communications and finance. Name the lines and you can see what Researcher skipped.

2

Citations are claims about claims

A citation proves where a sentence came from, not that it's true. Priya's "we should be covered" is a real, citable source, and it's wrong.

3

Count independent sources

A finding backed by one source is a hypothesis. A finding backed by a contract, a spreadsheet and an email is a conclusion. Ask Researcher to show the count.
