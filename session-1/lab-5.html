The mission

Make the call.

It's Wednesday, 11:00 AM. You have a decision timeline (Inning 2), a cited readiness brief (Inning 3) and a verified exception workbook (Inning 4). Sam's fan email deadline is 5:00 PM today. Leadership doesn't want three documents. They want one scorecard and one action plan.

In this inning you bring the evidence together, separate what you know from what you estimate and what's still unknown, and defend a recommendation: proceed, proceed with conditions, or delay.

☝ Call your shotPredict before you play. Wrong guesses teach the most.

Back to Inning 1's question, now with the evidence: how many of the five weekend elements are GREEN?

Two: Rally Towels and Fireworks. Mini bats are YELLOW (fixable today), bobbleheads are RED (600 short of contract) and bucket hats are RED (no contract, no PO, no stock). How close was your Inning 1 guess?

Game plan

  • Combine communication, research and spreadsheet findings into one view.
  • Separate verified facts, estimates and unresolved issues.
  • Prioritize risks by business impact.
  • Split decisions leadership can make now from those that need more information.
  • Deliver a readiness scorecard and prioritized action plan.

🎙 Postgame press conferenceBig Idea #5 of 11
The big idea

Separate what you know from what you think

A decision brief is a sorting exercise. Facts get a source, estimates get a method, unknowns get an owner and a deadline. Then the irreversible decisions get the highest bar.

1

Fact · estimate · unknown

Each one needs different handling: facts are cited, estimates come with a method and a range, and unknowns are assigned to someone with a deadline. Mixing them is how a guess turns into a plan.

2

Decide at the right resolution

It isn't "go or no-go on the weekend." Decide per element: proceed, proceed with conditions, or delay. Most real decisions are conditional.

3

Reversibility sets the bar

An email to 180,000 fans can't be recalled, so it needs the strongest evidence. Rescheduling a pallet is cheap and reversible, so just do it.

Dugout notes

Dugout notes · instructor answer key

Timing (15 min): 2 min sort facts/estimates/unknowns (demo only) · 3 min risk register and grid · 2 min now vs later · 5 min vote and defense · 3 min scorecard reveal and bridge to Game 2. If you're short on time, skip step 3 and show the reference scorecard.

Expected votes and the strongest defenses:

  • Bucket hats → Delay (nearly unanimous). Evidence: E04 is conditional, E08 has no Ops sign-off, E11 changed the scope, no PO (E07), zero stock. Physically impossible by Sunday without a rush, and the fan email would promise it to 180,000 people. A "proceed with conditions" team should be asked: "Name the condition that can be met by Wed 5:00 PM." There isn't one.
  • Bobbleheads → Proceed with conditions (most common). The game still happens and 14,400 fans get one. The condition is proactive sponsor notification with the make-good options (the Event Standards require reporting within 24 hours of the event, but telling them before is the relationship move). A "Delay" team is overreacting; push them on the cost of cancelling the sponsor's biggest night.

Discussion prompt: "Which finding would you have missed without the spreadsheet?" The answer is the bobblehead shortfall. Every communication said "we're covered."

Seed for Session 2: mention that Ryan at BRE still thinks the hats are approved (E05), and that Riverbend gets to choose credit or a replacement night. Both come back in the Plot Twist.
