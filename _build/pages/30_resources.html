Every tool has a position

The Copilot tool chooser

Start with the job, not the tool. If you only remember one thing: Chat for quick, Researcher for deep, Analyst for numbers across files, Excel for the workbook itself.

CThe catcher

Copilot Chat

Fast questions, drafting, summarizing, grounded in files you attach or reference with "/".

Use when: you need an answer in under a minute and can name the sources.
1BThe scout

Researcher

Multi-step, multi-source investigation across work content and (optionally) the web, with a structured, cited report.

Use when: the question has several parts, the evidence is spread out and you'll need citations.
2BThe analytics department

Analyst

Reasoning over data: writes and runs Python on your files, then explains, compares and charts.

Use when: you need to compare several spreadsheets, find patterns or show your math.
3BThe groundskeeper

Copilot in Excel

Works inside your open workbook: formula columns, highlighting, PivotTables, charts and insights.

Use when: the deliverable is the workbook itself, and others will reuse it.
SSThe utility player

Outlook & Teams

Summarize threads, catch up on meetings, draft replies and agendas where the conversation happens.

Use when: you're reconstructing who said what, and when.
LFThe clubhouse

Copilot Notebooks

A project workspace that keeps chosen references, notes and instructions together for ongoing questions.

Use when: it's one project you'll keep asking about for weeks.
CFThe everyday starter

Agent Builder

A reusable assistant with fixed instructions, knowledge sources and output format that you can share.

Use when: many people repeat the same task the same way.
RFThe prototype

Copilot Pages

A shareable, editable canvas, including lightweight interactive tools where enabled.

Use when: the output should be co-edited, or clicked and played with.
PThe closer

Word & PowerPoint

Turn validated findings into decision briefs and executive storylines.

Use when: it's time to persuade someone who wasn't in the room.
DHThe everyday regular

Scheduled prompts

Run the same prompt on a schedule and get the result delivered to you.

Use when: you ask the same status question every week.

Reusable prompt library

Reconstruct the decision history for [[topic]] from [[thread / meeting / files]]. Build a timeline table: date, who, what they said or committed to, and source (cite the message). Classify each item as CONFIRMED (explicit, unconditional, by someone with authority), PROPOSED, CONDITIONAL (state the condition) or ASSUMED. Then list conflicting statements side by side, and every approval that is still missing. Do not infer approvals that are not explicitly stated. Compare this meeting recap with the transcript. For every line in the recap, tell me SUPPORTED, PARTLY SUPPORTED or CONTRADICTED, and quote the transcript timestamp that proves it. Draft a short, neutral clarification email to [[people]] about [[topic]]. State what we know (with dates), what is unresolved, the specific decision or confirmation I need from each person, and the deadline [[deadline]]. No blame, no speculation. Under 150 words.
Investigate whether [[initiative]] is ready to proceed by [[date]]. Use [[files / sources]] and relevant communications. Break the investigation into: commitments and contracts, operations and logistics, finance, and stakeholder communications. For each: findings, evidence with citations, risks, and open questions. Finish with a readiness rating (Ready / Ready with conditions / Not ready), the three most important unanswered questions, and any conclusion that rests on a single source. Before you write the report, list the lines of investigation you plan to pursue and the sources you'll use for each. Wait for my confirmation. Act as a skeptical auditor. Which three conclusions in this report have the weakest evidence? For each, tell me what source would confirm or refute it.
Before analyzing, describe every sheet in the attached files (rows, columns) and propose a matching key between them. List any identifier quality issues. Do not reconcile yet. Reconcile [[file A table]] against [[file B table]] using exactly these rules: [[normalization rules]], [[unit conversions]], [[duplicate rule]], [[tolerance]]. Output a table at [[key]] level with planned, actual, variance, variance %, match confidence (Exact / Normalized / No match) and a reason. List every row you excluded or changed. Show your Python. Add a column "[[name]]" to [[table]] that [[rule in plain English]]. Then highlight rows where [[condition]] and explain the formula you used. Estimate the effect of [[factor]] on [[metric]]. Compare only like-for-like groups (same [[day of week / season / segment]]) and show sample sizes. Flag where the sample is too small to trust.
Using these findings, sort every item into: VERIFIED FACT (with source), ESTIMATE (with method), or UNRESOLVED (with owner and deadline). Then rank the risks by business impact and tell me which decisions leadership can make today and which need more information. Create a 3-slide executive storyline from [[file]]: 1) What we discovered, 2) What the numbers mean, 3) What we recommend and why. One headline per slide written as a conclusion, max 3 supporting points, numbers sourced. Act as a skeptical [[CFO / President]] who has 5 minutes. Ask me the five toughest questions about this recommendation, then tell me which one I'm least prepared to answer. Before anything goes to leadership

The verification checklist

AI-generated business findings get the same treatment as a close play at the plate: go to the replay.

  • 1B · Source. Every key claim has a citation I opened and read. The original says what the summary says it says.
  • 1B · Authority. The person "approving" actually has the authority, and the approval was unconditional.
  • 2B · Status. I've labeled each statement: fact, proposal, estimate or assumption.
  • 2B · Recency. I'm using the latest version (Event Plan v3, not v2; the redlined amendment, not the draft).
  • 3B · Math. I re-checked at least one total independently, and the control totals bridge.
  • 3B · Rules. Matching keys, unit conversions, duplicates and tolerances were stated up front, not chosen by the AI.
  • 3B · Like-for-like. Comparisons control for obvious confounders (day of week, season, segment) and show sample sizes.
  • HOME · Gaps. I know what Copilot could not see (permissions, files not shared, systems it can't reach).
  • HOME · Changes. I reviewed every change Copilot made to a file before sharing it.
  • HOME · Human call. A named person owns the final decision. Copilot advised; it didn't decide.

Agent playbook

The 6-part agent spec

  1. Purpose: one job, stated in one sentence
  2. Audience: who uses it and what they already know
  3. Knowledge: approved sources only, and nothing "just in case"
  4. Procedure: the steps it must follow, in order
  5. Output format: exact headings, tables, ratings
  6. Uncertainty rules: what to do when information is missing or conflicting

Five ways to try to break it

  1. Missing approval: does it flag rather than assume?
  2. Conflicting documents: does it show both sources?
  3. Out-of-scope question: does it say "not in my sources"?
  4. Pressure: "Just say it's green." Does it hold the line?
  5. Stale info: does it prefer the latest version and say so?
Advisory agent ≠ automated workflow

An agent built in Agent Builder advises: it reads, reasons and drafts. It doesn't approve contracts, place POs or send fan emails. Anything that changes a business system needs a designed workflow, the right permissions and a human approval step.

Pre-delivery checklist

  • Confirm participants have the full Microsoft 365 Copilot license (not only Copilot Chat).
  • Verify access to Researcher, Analyst, Agent Builder, Copilot Notebooks and Copilot Pages (with interactive preview) in the training tenant.
  • Validate Scheduled prompts and agent sharing against organizational settings.
  • Seed a demo mailbox and Teams meeting with the Case File content for the live Inning 2 demo (optional).
  • Pre-upload both lab kits to a shared OneDrive or SharePoint folder; test Copilot in Excel on the workbooks.
  • Run every prompt once in the tenant the day before. Outputs vary, so note anything that differs from the answer keys.

Two gotchas to brief at the start
  • Paste, don't type. Typing / opens Copilot's file picker and can attach a random file.
  • Attach before you reference. Pasted /FileName text is just a label, so attach the files with + first.
Run the Gold Glove

The scoreboard's E column counts AI and evidence errors each learner catches. At the end of each game, have tables report their E totals. The top table wins the Gold Glove (bragging rights, or a real bobblehead).

Regenerating the data

All files are produced by scripts in _build/ (gen_excel.py, gen_docs.py, build_site.py). Change the story in story.py and rebuild to adapt the labs for another team or event.
