The mission

The Missing Approval

A sponsorship email says an additional giveaway, 5,000 Season Ticket Member bucket hats from Big River Energy on Sunday 6/20, has been approved. An operations meeting suggests it's still pending. The fan email that mentions "Bucket Hat Sunday" sends to 180,000 people Thursday at 10:00 AM, and Sam needs a go/no-go by Wednesday 5:00 PM.

Your job: use Copilot to reconstruct what actually happened across 16 emails and one Teams huddle, separate confirmed decisions from proposals and assumptions, and catch the AI-generated meeting recap that got it wrong.

Game plan

  • Reconstruct a business decision from fragmented communications and trace how it changed.
  • Spot conflicting statements, revised commitments and missing approvals.
  • Tell confirmed decisions apart from proposals and assumptions, with evidence.
  • Check an AI meeting recap against the transcript.
  • Draft a clarification email, meeting agenda and follow-up actions.

The play-by-play

  1. Get the lay of the land

    I've attached an export of 16 emails (IDs E01-E16) and a Teams meeting transcript with an auto-generated recap, all about the Riverfront Rally Weekend promotion (Fri 6/18 - Sun 6/20, 2027). Give me a 6-bullet briefing: the main topics discussed, who the key people are and their roles, and which topics look unresolved. Cite email IDs or transcript timestamps for every bullet.
    What you should see
    • Topics: the bucket hat add-on, magnets delivered after cancellation, bobblehead damage, the fan email deadline, finance's question about the $25K.
    • People: Dana, Jordan, Elena, Marcus, Priya, Sam, Tasha, Chris, and Ryan at the sponsor.

  2. Follow one decision through time

    Now narrow in on the disputed giveaway and build a timeline.

    Build a timeline of every message and meeting moment that concerns the Big River Energy bucket hat giveaway (PR-1057). Table columns: Date/time | Source (email ID or transcript timestamp) | Who | What they said or did (quote the key phrase) | Type: Proposal / Conditional approval / Claim of approval / Objection / Scope change / Action item | Did this change the status? (yes/no and how) Order it chronologically. After the table, state in one sentence what the status of the bucket hat giveaway is as of the end of the June 15 meeting, and why.
    What you should see
    • Roughly 12-20 rows (Copilot is often more granular than you'd expect) from E01 (the rule) through E03, E04, E05-E07, E08, E09/E10, E11, E15, E16 and the 00:02:44-00:04:33 transcript exchange.
    • Status: pending / not approved. Two of the three required approvals are missing, and the sponsor changed the scope.
    Scouting report · Quote the key phrase

    Asking for the quoted phrase makes the difference between "Elena approved" and "Looks good to me pending Marcus confirming…" impossible to miss. Quotes make claims checkable.

  3. Confirmed, proposed or assumed?

    Using the approval rule in E01 (a new giveaway needs ALL THREE: a signed sponsor amendment, Elena's approval, and Marcus's Ops capacity sign-off), evaluate the bucket hat giveaway. For each of the three requirements, give: Met / Not met / Conditional, the evidence (email ID or timestamp with a short quote), and any statement that contradicts it. Then list every statement in the emails or meeting that claims the giveaway is approved, and explain whether the evidence supports that claim. Do not soften conclusions. If a claim is unsupported, say so.
    What you should see
    • Signed amendment: Not met. Unsigned at lock (E09/E10); the sponsor's redline in E11 changes the scope; "basically done" at 00:03:24.
    • Elena's approval: Conditional (E04: "pending Marcus… and the amendment coming back signed").
    • Ops sign-off: Not met (E08 "I have NOT signed off"; 00:02:58).
    • Unsupported approval claims: E05 (to the sponsor!), E06, E10, and 00:02:44.

  4. Umpire review: check the recap against the transcript

    The transcript file includes an auto-generated recap that nobody reviewed. Ask Copilot to audit it, then check each line yourself.

    The transcript document contains a "Meeting recap (auto-generated - not reviewed)" section followed by the full transcript. Audit the recap line by line against the transcript. For each recap bullet return: the recap claim | Supported / Partly supported / Contradicted | the transcript timestamp(s) and quote that prove it | the corrected wording. Be strict: a recap line that drops a condition or reverses a decision counts as Contradicted.

    Now be the umpire. Tick each recap error you confirm in the transcript:

    "Saturday: Bobbleheads delivered - more than 15,000 on hand. Ready."
    Hint: what did Priya actually say at 00:01:30, and what did E12 say about damage?
    "Bucket hat giveaway approved by Elena (Jordan Pierce)."
    Hint: what did Marcus conclude at 00:04:33?
    "Action - Sam: send the fan email Thursday as planned."
    Hint: what was Sam actually asked to do?
    Umpire review · Recaps compress, and compression drops conditions

    Recaps are useful starting points, but they're summaries of a summary. Before you forward one or act on it, check any line that contains a decision, a number or an owner against the transcript.

  5. Build the outstanding-commitments report

    Create an outstanding-commitments report from the emails and the transcript (not the recap). Include every commitment, request, or open question, not just the bucket hats: e.g., the magnet cancellation, purchase orders, sponsor scope, staffing, reconciliation, the fan email, finance questions, and damaged inventory. Table columns: # | Commitment or open item | Owner (or "NO OWNER") | Due date/time (or "none stated") | Source | Current status (Done / Open / Overdue / Failed) | Risk if missed Sort by due date, earliest first. Flag in bold any item that has no owner or whose deadline has already passed as of Wed June 16, 2027 9:00 AM.
    What you should see
    • Owners and deadlines from 00:04:33: Jordan (amendment + scope), Marcus (staffing by Wed noon), Priya (expedite check + reconciliation), Sam (hold the hat line, go/no-go Wed 5 PM per E15).
    • Failed: the magnet cancellation (E02 sent 5/18, but magnets were delivered 6/14 per E13/E14).
    • Overdue: the hat PO deadline of May 28 (E07, no reply).
    • No owner: returning magnets to the warehouse; working out how many bobbleheads are unusable (E12); finance's BRE question (E16).

  6. Draft the follow-up: email + agenda

    Draft a clarification email from Dana Whitfield to Jordan Pierce, Elena Ortiz and Marcus Bell (cc Sam Kowalski and Chris Ahn). Purpose: get a definitive go/no-go on the Big River Energy bucket hats before the fan email content deadline (Wed 6/16 5:00 PM). Requirements: - Neutral, factual, non-blaming tone. Under 200 words. - State the approval rule from E01 and list which of the three approvals are and aren't met, with the email ID for each. - Flag that the sponsor's 6/9 redline (E11) changes the scope from 5,000 to 10,000 hats for the same $25,000. - Ask each person one specific question with a deadline. - Say that until all three approvals are met, the bucket hat line will be removed from the fan email and the $25K stays out of the forecast. Then draft a 15-minute agenda for a 12:15 PM Wednesday decision huddle, with time boxes, decision owner and required inputs for each item.
    Scouting report · Make Copilot ask, not assume

    Add "Before drafting, list any facts you'd need that aren't in the sources." Copilot may notice it doesn't know whether staffing was confirmed at noon. That's a perfect line for the agenda.

  7. Check the originals

    Click the citations in Copilot's last two answers. For at least three claims, open the source and confirm the wording. In particular, confirm with your own eyes:

    • E04: Elena's exact conditional wording.
    • E11: the sponsor's redline to 10,000 hats. Was it ever forwarded to Dana?
    • 00:04:33: Marcus's "we treat them as pending."
